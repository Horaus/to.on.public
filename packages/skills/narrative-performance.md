# Narrative Performance and Video Grammar

This is the composable creative/content layer. Infer independent purpose, driver, duration, seriality, dialogue, pacing, motion, physical-law, environment and sound axes before selecting only relevant modules. Never equate runtime with genre or force a pure genre template onto a hybrid work. It never changes locked facts, ownership, causal order, outcomes, continuity or technical contracts.

## story

- Define one dramatic question that can be answered by a visible choice and consequence, not by exposition alone.
- Build an emotional arc as changing tactics under pressure. Escalation must invalidate or increase the cost of the previous tactic; repeating the same warning louder is not escalation.
- Choose one story-bearing motif: a prop, sound, gesture, spatial relationship, or repeated visual condition. Give it a distinct setup, development, and payoff. The motif supports the story and must not introduce a new plot obligation.
- For every consequential character, lock a public want, emotional defense, pressure response, and recognizable voice pattern. Distinction comes from worldview, syntax, specificity, politeness, rhythm, and what the character avoids saying—not accent stereotypes or decorative catchphrases.
- A scene changes objective pressure, knowledge, relationship, or commitment. State its escalation mechanism, choice pressure, emotional shift, and motif function before writing dialogue.
- Dialogue is action. Each line uses a concrete tactic such as probe, deflect, bargain, corner, reassure, conceal, challenge, concede, command, or reframe. Give it subtext and a relationship/information delta.
- Prefer behavior with an interpretable intention. Performance direction should name playable behavior under pressure, not generic emotions such as 'sad', 'intense', or 'cinematic'.
- Silence is permitted only for a readable reaction, withheld answer, power shift, or motif payoff. Silence cannot carry a proposition that the narrative contract marks `mustBeSpoken`.
- Treat dialogue exchange, not an isolated line, as the unit: every reply answers, resists, reframes or escalates a projected prior turn and changes information, trust, power or commitment.
- Treat a shot as a purposeful perceptual/dramatic setup, not as a synonym for one action. Preserve state change: incoming state → stimulus → response/action → visible outgoing state.
- Design coverage as a pressure curve across adjacent shots. Establish readable geography, then increase proximity, obstruction, eyeline pressure, or motivated movement only as choices narrow; reserve the strongest justified visual pressure for decision/climax and release it after consequence.
- Build and pass the shared live-action film grammar first: playable intention, actor blocking, spatial continuity, motivated coverage, reaction, cut point and sequence-level dramatic progression. Animation is an optional expression module layered on this foundation; it must never replace or relax it.
- Activate animation-specific pose, exaggeration, stylized-physics or silent-comedy rules only when the source or user explicitly asks for animation/stylization. Do not infer animation merely from energetic action, comedy, short runtime or a nonhuman character.
- When animation, visual comedy, silent, pose-led or environment-driven modules are active, externalize want, attention, cause, reaction, residue and expectation. Lock an internal physical/style rule, motion vocabulary and exaggeration ceiling per character. Prefer readable pose, gaze, anticipation, impact and recovery; the environment may act as a causal system.
- Stylized or impossible motion is valid when comprehension, established physical/style rules, character identity and consequence remain consistent. Smoothness and photographic realism are not universal quality criteria.
- The ending must transform the meaning of an earlier motif, choice, or behavior. A confirmation line alone is not a satisfying final image when the required outcome is visual.

## review

- Reject scenes whose pressure repeats without changing cost, knowledge, relationship, or available choices.
- Reject dialogue that could be swapped between speakers without changing vocabulary, tactic, subtext, or power relationship.
- Reject lines that label visible action, generic reaction fragments, repeated information, or emotional claims unsupported by behavior.
- Reject sequences whose dialogue escalates while framing, distance, blocking and visual pressure remain interchangeable. Variation must express changing dramatic access, not decorative angle churn.
- Confirm the motif has setup, development and payoff and remains subordinate to the causal story.
- Confirm each scene enters and exits with a different dramatic state and the final image answers the dramatic question.
- Reject animation or stylization that masks weak blocking, unclear coverage, broken continuity, dead dramatic progression or an unreadable edit. Passing a style-specific motion check never compensates for failing the shared film grammar.
- Evaluate only active profile dimensions. Do not flag a purposeful comic pause, selective/stepped motion, impossible camera or stylized physics using a live-action realism baseline.
- For visual/animated work, run a silent pass: want, attention, cause, reaction, state change and next expectation must remain readable. Reject dead holds, weak pose hierarchy, motion-vocabulary drift, camera/action competition and environment resets without an established rule.
