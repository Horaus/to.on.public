# Supplement Health Short Video

Use this skill for short social videos about dietary supplements, nutrition, growth, or general wellness.

## Story

- Open with a concrete visual hook in the first 1-3 seconds.
- Keep one central message for a 20-45 second video.
- Distinguish food, dietary supplements, and medicine.
- Do not promise height gain, rapid growth, cure, guaranteed results, or universal effectiveness.
- Do not imply that one product replaces sleep, balanced nutrition, exercise, or professional medical advice.
- State that results depend on age, nutrition status, health, and individual needs.
- For children, pregnancy, chronic disease, medication use, or suspected deficiency, recommend consulting a qualified clinician.
- Avoid invented statistics, fabricated studies, and unsupported ingredient claims.
- Present label reading, dosage compliance, contraindications, and trusted sourcing as practical actions.

Use this structure: hook with one misconception, context, three concise checks or actions, safety note, and a neutral call to action.

## Prompt

- Prefer observable actions: comparing labels, preparing a balanced meal, sleeping, exercising, or speaking with a clinician.
- Use on-screen text for key checks, not dense medical paragraphs.
- Keep shots independently generatable and 2-4 seconds long.
- Avoid before-and-after body transformations and misleading product demonstrations.

## Keyframe

- Show one concrete action and one health idea per frame.
- Keep labels, dosage, ingredients, and product packaging faithful to approved references.
- Do not visualize guaranteed outcomes, body transformations, or fabricated clinical evidence.

## Video

- Animate the observable action only; do not add claims, labels, products, or outcomes absent from the keyframe.
- Preserve packaging, dosage context, character identity, and safety cues from the attached reference.
- Keep movement restrained enough that health information remains visually unambiguous.

## Review

- Reject unsupported claims, misleading transformations, altered product labels, unsafe dosage implications, and invented evidence.
- Confirm that the visual result still distinguishes supplements from medicine and does not replace professional advice.
